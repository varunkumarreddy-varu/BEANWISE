1. Introduction and Problem Definition

Agricultural seed certification and quality assurance demand objective, rapid, and repeatable grain classification. In the production of dry beans (Phaseolus vulgaris L.), human visual grading is prone to subjective bias, fatigue, and error due to the close physical resemblance of cultivars. BEANWISE provides an automated computer vision-based machine learning solution that classifies seven commercial dry bean varieties using 16 physical geometric and morphological dimensions.

2. Data Preparation and Cleaning (Phase 3)

The raw dataset was verified for cryptographic integrity using SHA-256 (a9efa69741c6c5d95167c962a13da23a456f7e940402a5add12639790e01f714). Data auditing confirmed zero missing values, zero null entries, and zero non-finite numbers across all 13,611 rows. Exactly 68 duplicate records (0.50%) were identified and removed with scientific justification to eliminate artificial data leakage between training and testing partitions, yielding a cleaned dataset of 13,543 records. Biological outliers (e.g. Area > 150,000 pixels) were retained after confirming they represent the naturally massive Bombay cultivar.

3. Feature Engineering and Selection (Phase 4)

Six domain morphological indicators were formulated to capture shape ratios and surface irregularities: Area_to_Perimeter, Axis_Ratio, Axis_Difference, Convex_Area_Ratio, Compactness_Roundness_Ratio, and EquivDiameter_to_MajorAxis. Multi-criteria feature selection evaluated Mutual Information, ANOVA F-tests, Random Forest Gini importance, and Recursive Feature Elimination strictly on the 70% training partition, isolating top discriminative indicators (notably ShapeFactor1, Perimeter, Area, and Compactness).

6. Error Analysis and Observations

Error analysis showed that cultivars with distinct dimensional scale (Bombay) or extreme aspect ratios and roundness (Horoz, Seker) achieve near-flawless classification ($F1 \ge 0.95$). The primary source of error is between Dermason and Sira, where biological size distributions naturally overlap. This demonstrates the integrity of the evaluation, reflecting true biological variation rather than overfitted artifacts.

7. Web Application and Real-Time Serving

The platform serves predictions via a streamlined Streamlit web application. The application opens directly to the prediction form, validating manual user inputs, deriving the 6 domain features, and generating real-time predictions with dynamic specimen photographs, model-estimated probability distributions, and advisory range warnings. Bulk batch CSV classification and botanical gallery features are conditionally accessible.
